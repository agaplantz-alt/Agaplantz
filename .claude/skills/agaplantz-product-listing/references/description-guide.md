# Description guide

## The shape

Four paragraphs, all `<p>`, no headings:

1. **What it is.** Open with the plant's full name as the subject, then the single trait it's known for. One or two sentences. The reader should know in ten words why this plant is on a rare-plant site.
2. **What it's like to own.** How the trait shows up over time — new growth colour, how the variegation reads, leaf texture, how it fills a space. Concrete and sensory. One to three sentences.
3. **How it grows.** Habit (climbs, crawls, stays compact), how it matures, what changes with age. One or two sentences. Skip any point you're not sure of.
4. **Disclaimer, in bold.** One of the two closers below, verbatim.

Total: 60–110 words. Long descriptions don't sell rare plants; the photo does. The text's job is to confirm the buyer's identification and set honest expectations.

**Pack listings** get one extra bold paragraph between the body and the closer, so nobody buys a 5-pack thinking it's a single plant:

```html
<p><strong>Ships as a set of five tissue-culture plantlets.</strong> This variety is grown and shipped in sealed multiples rather than as single plants, so the pack is priced well below five singles.</p>
```

Adjust the number and keep the second sentence — it answers the "why can't I buy one" question before it reaches the support inbox.

## Closers

Variegated, coloured, or pink/mint/albo/aurea anything:

```html
<p><strong>Leaf coloration and variegation may naturally vary depending on lighting, maturity, and growing conditions.</strong></p>
```

Non-variegated:

```html
<p><strong>Each plant is unique — size, shape, and leaf count vary naturally. Photos are representative of the variety, not the exact plant shipped.</strong></p>
```

## Voice

Plant person to plant person. Confident, specific, warm, no hype words doing the work of facts.

- **Do** use the vocabulary collectors use: fenestrations, albo, aurea, sectoral, velvet, jewel Alocasia, crawler, climber, mature form.
- **Do** name colours precisely: neon-yellow, chartreuse, creamy-white, copper, bronze, deep green. "Stunning" and "gorgeous" tell the reader nothing they can't see in the photo.
- **Do** say "collectible", "sought-after", "rare" when true — these are rare-plant buyers and the words are accurate.
- **Don't** open with "Introducing", "Elevate your space", or "Looking for". The plant name is the first word.
- **Don't** mention price, shipping, pre-order timing, or the lab in the description. Those live on the pre-order page and shipping policy, and they change.
- **Don't** claim anything about variegation stability, growth rate, or "guaranteed" traits.
- **Don't** give care instructions here. The acclimation guide covers it; care in a listing implies a promise. One exception: for a genuinely demanding plant (Warocqueanum, most velvet Anthuriums), one sentence of expectation-setting is fair — *"Demanding but not impossible — it wants consistently high humidity and an open mix."* That's a warning, not a how-to; stop there.

## Real examples from the store

**Monstera Electro Light** (variegated, climber):

```html
<p>Monstera Electro Light is one of the most exciting modern variegates, famous for the electric, neon-yellow new growth that practically glows against its mature deep green leaves.</p>
<p>Bright, bold, and a guaranteed conversation starter, the Electro Light delivers vivid colour without the unpredictability of classic variegation. Every fresh leaf emerges in a dazzling chartreuse before settling to green.</p>
<p>As the plant climbs and matures, it develops the signature Monstera fenestrations, with new growth continuing to flash that unmistakable electric glow.</p>
<p><strong>Leaf coloration and variegation may naturally vary depending on lighting, maturity, and growing conditions.</strong></p>
```

**Alocasia Melo Albo Variegated** (variegated, compact) — opening line as it appears on the store:

> Alocasia Melo Albo Variegated is a compact, jewel-like Alocasia, prized for its crisp, creamy-white albo variegation. An eye-catching, collectible piece…

**Philodendron Gloriosum Variegated** (variegated, crawler) — opening line:

> Philodendron Gloriosum Variegated is a crawling aroid legend, treasured for its large, velvety, heart-shaped leaves and luminous white veining…

Notice the pattern: `[Full name] is a [habit/category] [genus], [prized/famous/treasured] for its [one concrete trait].` Reuse the pattern, vary the verbs.

## Worked example — a new listing

Input: "Anthurium Clarinervium, pre-order $38, not variegated, MOQ 20."

```html
<p>Anthurium Clarinervium is a compact velvet-leaf Anthurium, prized for its deep green, heart-shaped foliage cut through with bold, pale-silver veins.</p>
<p>Each leaf has a soft, suede-like texture, and the vein pattern sharpens as the leaf hardens off, so a well-grown plant reads almost like a line drawing.</p>
<p>It stays tidy and low-growing rather than climbing, with leaves gaining size as the plant matures.</p>
<p><strong>Each plant is unique — size, shape, and leaf count vary naturally. Photos are representative of the variety, not the exact plant shipped.</strong></p>
```

## SEO meta description

One sentence, ≤ 155 characters, plant name first:

> Anthurium Clarinervium tissue culture plants from AgaPlantz — rare velvet-leaf aroid, lab-grown and shipped across Canada. Pre-order from the next batch.

Count the characters; Google truncates, and a cut-off sentence looks careless.
