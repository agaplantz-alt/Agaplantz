## 3. CRITICAL — Tracking is broken in three places

This is the top of the decision hierarchy. Every performance number in this account is currently unreliable.

### 3.1 The Meta pixel has never fired. Not once.

```
Dataset: "Agaplantz" (4340434602872984)
Created:            2026-08-15
last_fired_time:        1969-12-31T16:00:00-0800   <-- Unix epoch = NEVER
server_last_fired_time: 1969-12-31T16:00:00-0800   <-- no CAPI either
```

Epoch-zero timestamps mean zero events received, browser-side **and** server-side. The dataset exists, is active, and has first-party cookies enabled — it is simply not connected to the storefront.

**Consequence:** Meta advertising cannot function. No optimisation signal, no retargeting audience, no lookalike seed, no purchase attribution. Any Meta spend before this is fixed is spend into a blindfold.

### 3.2 Google Ads counts free $0 products as purchases

The "Tissue Culture Starter Kit" is given away free — **71 orders, $856 gross, $856 discounted, $0 net revenue.**

- That is **42.5% of every order the store has ever taken.**
- **28 of those free orders came from the `search` referrer** (i.e. largely Google Ads traffic).

Google Ads' only primary conversion action is `Google Shopping App Purchase` (82.67 conversions, $12,140 value). The free-kit orders are real Shopify orders, so they fire purchase events with ~$0 value.

**Consequence:** the conversion *count* Google optimises against is inflated by roughly a third with zero-revenue events. Under value-based bidding this partly self-corrects, but the count still drives learning-phase exit, audience expansion, and — critically — the freebie-claimers land in the remarketing and Customer Match pools that feed PMax audience signals. **The algorithm is being taught that people who take free things are the customer.**

### 3.3 Google Ads over-reports revenue by 1.5–1.9x

Three independent measurements of the same period:

Shopify referrer data is weak (91 of 167 orders have a blank referrer), so 1.86x is the loose bound and **1.53x against GA4 is the defensible figure**. Either way, Google is claiming credit for a meaningful share of orders that would have happened anyway. Real PMax ROAS is plausibly **~2.1x, not 3.2x** (Inference).

## 4. The biggest conversion leak: checkout

August funnel:

```
5,003 sessions
  ->   328 added to cart      (6.6% of sessions)
  ->   180 reached checkout   (54.9% of carts   -- healthy)
  ->    46 completed          (25.6% of checkouts -- BAD)
```

Cart→checkout is fine. **Checkout→purchase is 25.6%.** Typical e-commerce checkout completion runs ~45–50%, so AgaPlantz is roughly **half the norm** at the very last step. 134 people per month walk away with their payment details half-entered.

Fixing checkout completion from 25.6% to 40% at current traffic is **+26 orders/month at ~$212 paid AOV ≈ +$5,500/month revenue with zero additional ad spend.** No campaign optimisation available to this account comes close to that.

### Why (evidence-backed, ordered by likelihood)

**1. No real live-arrival guarantee — on $200–500 living goods.** The homepage promises a vague *"Live Plant Guarantee — We're here to help if your plant arrives damaged."* The shipping policy then withdraws it: *"Minor cosmetic imperfections, bent leaves, soil movement, or temporary wilting may occur during transit"*, no live arrival guarantee stated, and *"AgaPlantz is not responsible for orders delayed, lost, or returned due to incorrect or incomplete addresses."* Re-shipment may cost extra.

A collector about to spend $300 on a variegated Monstera shipped across Canada is buying a fragile living thing sight-unseen from a 4-month-old brand. Published collector research is consistent: buyers commit when they *trust the source*, and hesitate on *weak plant health signals* and *unclear terms*. The policy page is where trust dies. **This is the single most likely cause of the checkout leak.**

**2. Shipping-cost shock at the low end.** $14.99 standard / $25 express, free over $120. Paid AOV is ~$212 so most buyers clear the threshold — but Philodendrons start at $15, and $14.99 on a $15 plant is a **100% surcharge**. August shipping charges were $540.72 over 63 orders ($8.58 avg), confirming a large share do pay. Extra costs are the most-cited abandonment reason in published checkout research.

**3. Delivery uncertainty.** "2–8 business days," ships Mon–Wed only, four possible carriers, shipments delayed at AgaPlantz's discretion in heat or cold. Defensible operationally; unnerving at the payment step.

**4. Not yet ruled out:** express-wallet availability (Shop Pay / Apple Pay / Google Pay) at checkout, and whether pre-order items make the delivery date ambiguous. **I could not verify these** — they need a manual checkout walkthrough. See §7.

Two problems:

2. **Rolling end-dates train customers to wait.** Every deadline so far has been replaced by another. Scarcity that never bites stops working, and published collector research specifically flags that buyers *disengage* when rushed by scarcity language they don't believe.

## 7. What I could not determine

Stated plainly rather than guessed (PART 22):

1. **Contribution margin / COGS.** Not in any connected system. Without it, no true break-even ROAS or CAC ceiling can be set. **This is the single biggest blocker to running this account on profit rather than revenue.**
2. **Google Merchant Center feed health.** No connector. Feed quality is the dominant lever in PMax and is entirely unmeasured.
3. **Checkout page mechanics.** Express wallets, form length, pre-order date clarity — needs a manual walkthrough.
4. **Customer LTV / repeat interval.** Returning-customer rate is rising (8% → 24.5%) which is a strong signal, but only 4 months of history exists — too short for a real LTV curve.
5. **Whether the free Starter Kit converts to paying customers.** 71 people took it. Their subsequent purchase rate decides whether it is a smart acquisition tool or pure cost.
6. **Competitor ad activity.** AdWhispr free-tier calls exhausted. Google search terms did surface four Canadian competitors buyers compare against: **Planteia, Plantsome, Floral Acres, GardenWorks.**
