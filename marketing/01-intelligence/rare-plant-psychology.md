# Rare Plant Buyer Psychology — AgaPlantz specialisation

A rare-plant buyer is not a commodity e-commerce buyer. Generic DTC playbooks misfire here because they assume the purchase is a *transaction*. For a collector it is closer to an *acquisition*.

## The four buyer types (Hypothesis — to be validated against real order data)

| Type | Buys | Motivated by | Blocked by | AgaPlantz evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Collector** | $200–500 variegated specimens | Rarity, genetics, completing a collection | Transit risk, authenticity doubt | Monstera Devil Monster Premium Variegated $2,976 / 13 orders; Alocasia Cuprea Red Secret $2,112 / 11 orders |
| **The Propagator** | Tissue culture, pre-orders | Getting in early, cost per plant, the grow-out project | Uncertainty about TC success | `tissue culture plants canada` is our #1 search term; 71 free Starter Kits claimed |
| **The Aspirant** | $15–90 entry plants | Wanting one special plant | **$14.99 shipping on a $15 plant** | Philodendron Florida Ghost 18 orders / $492.82 |
| **The Flexer** | Whatever is trending | Status, visibility of the plant | Price without proof of rarity | Monstera Bulbasaur 29 orders — highest order count among paid plants |

**Do not market to all four the same way.** The Collector needs *risk removal*. The Propagator needs *anticipation management*. The Aspirant needs *shipping relief*. The Flexer needs *proof of rarity*.

## What actually drives the purchase

Published collector research converges on three simultaneous conditions: **they love the plant, they believe they can keep it alive, and they trust the seller.** All three must hold. Most rare-plant marketing addresses only the first.

AgaPlantz currently addresses the first well (good product photography, genuinely rare inventory), the second barely, and **the third is actively undermined by the shipping policy.**

### The trust stack, in the order a buyer builds it

1. **Does this plant exist as pictured?** — real photos of the actual plant, not stock; variegation is unique per plant, so a generic image reads as a red flag.
2. **Will it arrive alive?** — the gating fear. Currently unanswered: the policy explicitly disclaims transit damage and offers no live-arrival guarantee.
3. **Will it stay alive?** — acclimation support, care guidance, TC-to-soil transition.
4. **Is this seller still here in six months?** — pre-orders require believing the business will exist on delivery day. A 4-month-old brand must over-prove this.

## Scarcity: the one thing that is easy to get wrong

Collector research is explicit that buyers **disengage when rushed by scarcity language they don't believe.** Scarcity in plants is real and structural — variegated cuttings can't be grown from seed, so supply is genuinely time-bound. That is a *true* story and it works.

**AgaPlantz is currently telling a false version of it.** "Ends Aug 25," "Ends Aug 31" — rolling deadlines that keep being replaced. This trains exactly the behaviour we don't want: waiting for the next sale.

**Replace manufactured urgency with structural scarcity:**
- ❌ "20% off — ends Friday!" (repeats forever, trains waiting)
- ✅ "This batch: 12 plants. Next TC batch ~6 weeks." (true, verifiable, self-enforcing)
- ✅ "One plant, one photo — when it's gone, it's gone." (literally true of variegated specimens)

## Language: use theirs, not ours

Real search terms from our own account — this is the customer's vocabulary, captured from Tier 1 data:

> `tissue culture plants canada` · `monstera bulbasaur` · `variegated billietiae` · `monstera deliciosa aurea` · `alocasia black velvet pink variegated` · `where to buy plants online canada` · `monstera albo` · `white princess tricolor` · `cuprea true pink`

Note what they say and don't say. They use **cultivar names**, not categories. They say **"tissue culture,"** not "propagation material." They append **"canada"** constantly — domestic sourcing is a real anxiety (import/phytosanitary friction). They never say "houseplant."

**Implication:** ad copy, product titles and feed titles should lead with the cultivar name and include "Canada." Our highest-volume term already tells us the winning headline structure.
